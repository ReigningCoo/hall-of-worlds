# Vegetables, pulses and grains

Vegetables, pulses and grains are food you pick from plants: roots and tubers dug from the ground, leaves and stalks, pumpkins from vines, beans and peas in their pods, and sheaves of wheat, barley and rice. Each kind has its own page, with its picture, where it grows and what eating it raw does. Back to [Items](items).

## Finding and picking

Farms grow them in their fields, one crop to a field, choosing what suits the land. Crops sometimes grow wild too, though very rarely, and some plants (marked *wild* in the table below) are never farmed and only found wild, more often than escaped crops: a plant of the tundra won't grow in the desert.

A plant grows through four stages to full grown, as herbs do. Click or tap a full grown plant beside you to pick it. Picking cuts it back or picks it over, so it takes time to grow again; roots and tubers are dug up, and nothing grows there for a whole growth.

How many you get depends on the plant, and your **Foraging** and **Crops** skills find more. Picking practises Crops.

## Eating

Drag one onto your clothes, as you would other food, to eat it. Eaten raw, a vegetable gives back only a little Health, and only if people eat it raw: one that is seldom eaten raw, such as a potato, gives back none. A sheaf of grain gives back nothing as it is. Health comes back a little at a time, until you move or are hurt. A few also raise or lower an attribute for a while, or are harmful raw. The item's popup says what it does, and the effect shows in the bar at the top.

Vegetables can also be pressed into Neutral Oil, grain into Grain Oil, and dry beans, peas and other seeds into Seed Oil, for [alchemy](alchemy).

## Big vegetables

Most vegetables come one at a time or by the handful, and grain by the sheaf; the same kind stacks. A big one, such as a pumpkin, comes whole and takes more room, and a huge one, such as a yam, comes as a piece cut from it. When you eat it, it is cut into servings: you eat one, and the rest go back into your bags as slices (or onto the ground, if there is no room).

## Where they grow

Wild and on farms, by land. Those never farmed are marked.
