# Logs and kindling

Felling a tree ([woodcutting](woodcutting)) gives logs of its wood, which can be split into kindling. Back to [Items](items).

## Logs

A log is a length of trunk a metre long and about 30 cm across, its bark on the outside and the wood's rings showing at each end. It takes up 8 × 3 cells in your pack, and weighs what that much of its wood weighs, seasoned: from about 18 kg for the lightest woods to over 100 kg for the heaviest. Most are too heavy to carry more than one or two at a time.

Logs lying on the ground show from afar as one, two, or a pile of three or more.

## Kindling

Kindling is wood split into pieces of a kilogram each, keeping the wood it came from. Pieces of the same wood stack together, each taking up 2 × 1 cells.
