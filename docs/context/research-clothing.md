# Research: middle-layer clothing

What a generator can make for the Middle layer (everything worn over underclothes and under cloaks and armour, footwear apart), medieval and earlier first, in the terms the 3D models are built from. Done in November 2026 by the method in `research-underclothing.md`. Wikipedia's clothing and headgear categories gave 2,100 usable titles of 7,107; 230 names were ranked with the Google Books Ngram Viewer and 191 kept. 189 articles were read by Claude through the API, one extraction each, and every quote was checked against its article by script: 2,846 checked, 5 failed, 5 answers outside their lists. 29 overviews of period and regional dress were searched for garments without articles of their own: 807 named with checked quotes, 555 of them middle layer. The API drafted 32 fantasy pieces on the historical forms. Pictures are from Wikimedia Commons, chosen by hand from contact sheets (links only, below). Review page: https://claude.ai/artifact/Lgag7GEgsQUjaT7nP9fLRW (private).

Articles almost never give weights (1 of 189), so weights are worked out from cloth areas, as for underclothes.

## Owner's decisions

Before the research:

- Footwear is done (its own research); everything else on the Middle layer is in this one.
- Medieval and earlier first; later pieces rarer (see Q11).
- Cloaks, coats, surcoats and tabards belong to an Outer layer research (see Q1).
- Underclothes kept their own: the linen coif, wimple, hairnet and nightcap are not repeated here.

Answers to the review (2026-11-15: every recommendation taken):

1. **One garment on the torso:** Middle holds the main garment (tunic, kirtle, robe, doublet, bodice, vest). The ones worn over another (gowns, houppelandes, jerkins, haori, durumagi, kontusz, justacorps, kandys) go to the Outer research, so they clash with torso armour, as they would in life.
2. **Clothes under armour:** Keep the rule. A doublet may be made for arming (quilted, with points to tie the armour on), which goes under unlined plate as an arming vest does.
3. **Skirts and kilts:** Skirts, kilts, wraps and the dhoti list the Legs (Middle): they clash with trousers, not belts, and leg armour goes over them.
4. **Long tunics and gowns:** Tunics and robes list Torso and Arms only (Torso alone without sleeves), and their length is a property, as the shirt's is. So trousers, a skirt and a belt all go with any length.
5. **Hats:** Brimmed and stiff hats are Outer, over a hood, cap or veil. Hoods, soft caps, veils, turbans and headdresses are Middle. The hats are in this research, as it found them.
6. **Caps under turbans:** Skullcaps become a form of the Linen Coif (Skin), so a turban, hood or hat goes over them. Felt, fur and knitted caps stay Middle.
7. **Hoods and shoulders:** Hoods list Head and Neck; the cape's length is a property the model drapes over the shoulders. A hood and a shawl go together.
8. **Gloves:** One Gloves kind, rolled into a form: gloves, mittens or fingerless (fingerless on the Hands only, so rings stay free). Padded gloves stay with the armour.
9. **Belts:** One Belt kind rolled into those forms. The baldric (over the shoulder, to carry a sword) waits for sheaths.
10. **Sensitive garments:** Drop the Jewish hat. Keep the turban and veil as forms but not the names dastar, niqab or burqa. Keep the dalmatic as the Byzantine court tunic it was first; leave out the liturgical names. Keep a square headcloth with a cord, named Headcloth, not Keffiyeh.
11. **Later pieces:** Keep to 1700, marked later and rarer. Leave out the 19th century.
12. **Regional names:** Keep as specific names, as blades, footwear and underclothes did.
13. **Materials:** Velvet, Brocade and Damask as kinds of silk (and Velvet in wool); Tartan as a pattern, like stripes; Grass with Straw under plant materials; buckram and paste board only inside headdresses. Karakul is Fur.
14. **Tails, horns, wings and hooves:** No rule, as for underclothes: the model cuts the opening for whoever wears it. Birdfolk's wing-arms take no sleeves or gloves (they take only Skin there today).
15. **Hard mode:** Keep it so: Middle clothes drop on death on Hard, as they do now.

## What the sources agree on

- **Early medieval:** A wool or linen tunic to the knee, sleeves to the wrist, over a shirt, belted; trousers or hose with leg wraps; a hood or soft cap. Women: an ankle-length tunic, a veil or kerchief, a belt.
- **High medieval:** Men: a tunic (cotte) to the calf, girdled; the bliaut at court. Women: the bliaut, then the cotte or kirtle, and a veil with a wimple; a sideless surcoat over. Hoods with liripipes from the 14th century.
- **Late medieval:** Men: the fitted doublet (pourpoint) with hose pointed to it, the houppelande or jacket over it, the chaperon worn as a hat. Women: a fitted kirtle laced at the front or side, a gown over it, headdresses (hennin, escoffion), and the French and gable hoods by 1500.
- **16th and 17th century:** Doublets with padded bellies and wings, trunk hose and breeches, jerkins, ruffs then falling bands, bodices and partlets, felt hats (capotain, cavalier) and knitted Monmouth caps.
- **Ancient:** Greek chiton, peplos and exomis (draped and pinned rectangles, girdled with a zoster), the himation over; Roman tunica and stola; Egyptian shendyt kilts; Persian and Scythian trousers and sleeved coats; felt caps (pileus, Phrygian) and the petasos.
- **Elsewhere:** Japan: kosode and kimono with an obi, hakama. Korea: jeogori with baji or chima. China: the cross-collared yi with a qun skirt, or the one-piece shenyi. Steppe and Persia: the deel, kaftan and qaba over wide trousers, with a sash. India: kurta, dhoti, sari, choli and lehenga. Turbans, sashes and wrapped skirts from Africa to the Pacific.

## Building the models

- **Body points:** Every edge is a `body::Joint` level or a share between two, as for underclothes and armour, so a model scales with each people's frame. Thigh is halfway from Hip to Knee, Calf halfway from Knee to Ankle; Floor is the ankle plus a share of the foot's height.
- **Over the underclothes:** Every Middle piece clears the Skin layer (1 to 2 mm) and sits inside armour's 15 mm `STANDOFF_MM` wherever something Outer covers it. Its own ease shows only where it is uncovered: fitted 5 to 10 mm, loose 15 to 30, very full 40 to 120 (salvar, houppelande-like robes).
- **Skirts of garments:** Below the waist a tunic, robe or skirt is a cone from the hips: gored or pleated ones flare more; a tube (sarong) hangs straight; wraps overlap at the side. Over trousers it simply hides them. Trousers' legs under a long skirt are left out where covered at every place (`Inventory::shown`).
- **Belts:** A belt sits just outside whatever is Middle on the torso at the waist, cinching it (the tunic's model pulls in to the belt's line when one is worn). The long tongue end hangs at the front.
- **Headwear:** Hoods, caps and headcloths follow the skull with the hair under them; turbans are lofted rings; headdresses are stiff shapes from a few lofted rings with a hanging veil plane. Hats go over all of them on the Outer layer.
- **Bodies:** From the fantasy pieces: tail slits bound with tape or a buttoned flap, wraps overlapping at the back round the tail; hoods with horn sleeves or bound horn slits and a longer face opening for snouts; trousers cuffed and buckled above hooves, or tied above bare talons; wing-arms take sleeveless robes open at the sides, tied under the wings, and slit capelets (Q14).

- **Shepherd's Felt Cap** (cap, halfling): A soft rounded cone cap of brown felt with a rolled brim edge.

## Materials

- **In the game:** Linen, Wool, Silk, Cotton, Hemp, Ramie, Felt, Leather, Suede, Fur, Straw, Haircloth; lawn and net as finishes; the fantasy metals as thread, buttons and plates.
- **Missing (Q13):** Velvet, Brocade, Damask (silk, Velvet also wool); Tartan as a pattern; Grass with Straw; Buff (oiled oxhide) as a kind of leather.
- **Only inside:** Buckram, paste board, wire and whalebone, to stiffen headdresses, ruffs and bodices.

## Left out

- **Modern:** Blouses, sweaters, T-shirts, jeans, suits, cycling and sports wear, the qipao (1920s), harem pants, Victorian fashion.
- **Religious and imposed (Q10):** The Jewish hat; liturgical vestments (alb, chasuble, cincture and fascia as such, episcopal gloves); the dastar, niqab and burqa by name.
- **Not clothing yet:** Aprons (an Over piece, with the tabard), the baldric (with sheaths), chaps (Outer, with spurs), crowns and circlets (jewellery), masks.
- **Too slight:** Arm warmers and finger sleeves (modern); fillets and headbands go with jewellery.

## For the Outer layer research

Over-garments: gowns over kirtles, the houppelande, jerkins over doublets, justacorps, haori, durumagi, kontusz over żupan, chapan, kandys, the himation and other draped mantles, the belted plaid's upper half, cloaks, capes, ponchos and serapes, surcoats, tabards and aprons; gaiters and chaps over trousers; brimmed hats if Q5 goes the other way.
