# Clothing

Clothes are what you wear over your [underclothing](underclothing) and under cloaks and [armour](armour/). They go on the Middle layer. So far there are tunics, robes, doublets, bodices and vests for the torso, trousers and skirts for the legs, belts and gloves, and hoods, caps, headcloths, headdresses, neckwear and shawls. Hats go over all of these, on the Outer layer. They are what people wore in the Middle Ages and before, with the same pieces from other parts of the world and a few later ones, up to about 1700, which are rarer.

You wear one of them at a time on your torso. A long tunic or robe still covers only your torso and arms, however far down it hangs, so trousers and a belt go with any of them. Torso armour goes over them, unless it is lined: lined armour has its own padding, which takes the Middle layer too.

Trousers and skirts both cover your legs, so you wear one or the other; a belt goes with either, and leg armour goes over them. Every belt, whether a strap, a cord, a sash or an obi, has slots to tie pouches and bags to. Fingerless gloves leave your fingers free for rings.

A hood covers your head and neck, and its cape hangs over your shoulders, so a shawl still goes with it, unless the shawl has a collar. A cap, headcloth or headdress takes your head, and a headcloth that hangs to your shoulders takes your neck too. A hat goes over a hood, cap or headcloth, but not over a helmet. Skullcaps are [underclothes](underclothing), so a turban, hood or hat goes over one. Scarves and ruffs go over a necklace.

Some [mushrooms and lichens](fungi) harm whoever picks or handles them. Gloves keep off those that harm by touch. A bandana or a litham keeps out spores, as a closed helm or an aventail drawn over the face does.

On Hard, clothes are dropped when you die, as everything else you wear is, apart from underclothes.

Every piece is made in its own way. One that matches a garment from history takes its name, such as Kirtle or Kaftan. A name puts its pattern and an unusual material or weave first, as in "Striped Silk Robe" or "Velvet Doublet".

## Materials

Tunics are mostly wool or linen, robes silk, cotton or wool, doublets wool, silk or linen (arming doublets linen or cotton, and some doublets leather), bodices wool, linen or silk. Vests are cloth, leather, suede or buff leather, and some are lined with fur, as are some robes. Silk can be woven as velvet, brocade or damask, and wool as velvet too. Trousers are mostly wool or linen, some leather; skirts wool, linen, silk or cotton, wrapped ones mostly linen or cotton, and grass or sheepskin for the hanging kinds. Strap belts are leather or suede, girdles cord of wool, linen, silk or hemp, sashes and obis silk or cotton. Gloves are leather or suede when sewn, wool, silk or cotton when knitted, and felt or fur for some mittens. Hoods are mostly wool, caps wool, felt, knitted or fur, headcloths linen, cotton or silk, headdresses silk over a stiff frame, ruffs and bands linen, scarves and shawls mostly wool, and tippets fur. Hats are felt, straw or leather. Some tunics, robes, vests, trousers, skirts, sashes, kerchiefs, turbans, scarves and shawls are striped, checked or, in wool, tartan.
