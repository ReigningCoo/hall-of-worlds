# Clothing

Clothes are what you wear over your [underclothing](underclothing) and under cloaks and [armour](armour/). They go on the Middle layer. So far there are the torso's: tunics, robes, doublets, bodices and vests. They are what people wore in the Middle Ages and before, with the same pieces from other parts of the world and a few later ones, up to about 1700, which are rarer.

You wear one of them at a time on your torso. A long tunic or robe still covers only your torso and arms, however far down it hangs, so trousers and a belt go with any of them. Torso armour goes over them, unless it is lined: lined armour has its own padding, which takes the Middle layer too.

On Hard, clothes are dropped when you die, as everything else you wear is, apart from underclothes.

Every piece is made in its own way. One that matches a garment from history takes its name, such as Kirtle or Kaftan. A name puts its pattern and an unusual material or weave first, as in "Striped Silk Robe" or "Velvet Doublet".

## Kinds

| Kind | Covers | Goes with |
| ---- | ------ | --------- |
| Tunic | Torso and arms (torso only if sleeveless), from the neck to the hip, thigh, knee, calf, ankle or floor | A shirt under it; trousers and a belt |
| Robe | Torso and arms, from the neck to the knee, calf or ankle, closed at the front | A shirt under it; trousers and a belt |
| Doublet | Torso and arms, from the neck to the waist, hip or thigh, fitted | A shirt under it; hose or trousers; armour over it |
| Bodice | Torso, and the arms if it has sleeves, to the waist | A chemise under it; a skirt or petticoat |
| Vest | Torso, to the waist or hip, sleeveless | A shirt under it |

### Shape

| Kind | What it can be |
| ---- | -------------- |
| Tunic | Six lengths; cut from rectangles, widened with gores, fitted with the skirt pleated in, or a draped cloth pinned at the shoulders; sleeveless, short, elbow, wrist, wide, hanging or laced-on sleeves (draped ones pinned along the arms or over one shoulder); pulled on, laced at the front, side or back, or buttoned; a slit, buttoned, round, square, V or off-centre neck; plain, with woven bands, stripes from the shoulders, embroidery, smocking or woven panels |
| Robe | Knee, calf or ankle length; wrapped left over right or right over left, meeting at the centre, or with a curved flap; held by a sash, ties, buttons, side hooks or frogs; narrow, wide, square hanging or long hanging sleeves; a wrapped band, standing, round or no collar; unlined, lined, padded or fur-lined; plain, with contrasting edges, embroidered or patterned all over |
| Doublet | Waist, hip or thigh length; buttoned, laced, hooked or tied; unlined, lined, quilted, made for arming, or with a padded belly; no collar, a standing collar or a wrapped band; close, puffed, winged or detachable sleeves; plain, slashed, frogged with braid or with a contrasting lining; some have points at the waist to tie hose or armour to |
| Bodice | To the chest, the waist or below it in tabs; laced at the front, side or back, or tied; a square, round, high or low neck; no sleeves, short sleeves or laced-on sleeves; unstiffened, lined or boned; some with a stomacher panel at the front |
| Vest | Waist or hip length; cloth, leather, buff or fur-lined; buttoned, laced, hooked or open; plain, embroidered, braided or with contrasting edges |

## Materials

Tunics are mostly wool or linen, robes silk, cotton or wool, doublets wool, silk or linen (arming doublets linen or cotton, and some doublets leather), bodices wool, linen or silk. Vests are cloth, leather, suede or buff leather, and some are lined with fur, as are some robes. Silk can be woven as velvet, brocade or damask, and wool as velvet too. Some tunics, robes and vests are striped, checked or, in wool, tartan.

## Weights

Typical figures for an ordinary-sized piece in its usual material. Size and material change them: silk is lighter, wool, leather and velvet heavier, and fur linings add a lot.

| Kind | About |
| ---- | ----- |
| Tunic | 300 g to 1 kg |
| Robe | 1 to 2 kg; fur-lined ones up to about 3 kg |
| Doublet | 500 g to 1.5 kg |
| Bodice | 200 to 800 g |
| Vest | 300 g to 1 kg; a buff jerkin about 1.2 kg |
