# Jewellery

Jewellery is rings and necklaces. Both are worn against the skin, under your clothes and armour. Jewellers sell rings and necklaces, and so do gem cutters; the black market has the odd one too.

Every ring and necklace is made in its own way: its metal, its shape and the gems set in it. The better it is made, the more often it is of a fine metal and the more gems it has. Each gem is a whole gem of its own, with its own quality, held in a setting. A ring or necklace is named for its metal and what is set in it, as in "Gold Ring with Two Rubies".

## Kinds

| Kind | Worn on | Layer | How many |
| ---- | ------- | ----- | -------- |
| Ring | One finger | Skin | One on each finger |
| Necklace | Neck | Skin | One at a time |

Gloves and gauntlets go over your rings. Fingerless gloves and demi-gauntlets leave your fingers free, so you can still put rings on or take them off.

## Rings

A ring is a band of metal with up to five settings across its top.

| Part | What it can be |
| ---- | -------------- |
| Band | Domed, flat, round, square or knife-edged across; thin, medium or wide |
| Surface | Plain, hammered, engraved or twisted |
| Settings | None, one, two, three or five, each holding a gem in claws or in a rim of metal (a bezel) |

Most rings of plain metal have no settings. Now and then a setting is empty, or holds a gem a size too big for it.

## Necklaces

A necklace is a strand round the neck, sometimes with something hanging from it.

| Strand | What it is |
| ------ | ---------- |
| Cord | A leather thong: plain, braided or knotted, of leather, suede or rawhide |
| Chain | Fine, medium or heavy links, laid as a cable, rope, box or snake chain |
| Torc | A rigid twisted bar, open at the front, with shaped ends: plain, balls, buffers, scrolls or beasts' heads |

A necklace hangs close round the neck (a choker), on the collarbones, on the chest, or long. Torcs sit high, as chokers or on the collarbones.

What hangs from it is of the necklace's metal:

| Ornament | What it is |
| -------- | ---------- |
| Pendant | One gem in a setting, hung from a loop (the bail) |
| Amulet | A round, oval or eight-sided medallion with one gem in the middle, or three, four or six round its rim |
| Stations | Three, five or seven small settings spaced along the front of a chain |
| Charm | A small metal shape: a disc, teardrop, leaf, crescent, acorn, key, tooth, knot or shield |

Torcs have nothing hanging from them, and only chains have stations.

A necklace is named for what hangs from it, or else for its strand: "Silver Chain", "Leather Cord with an Iron Leaf", "Gold Pendant with a Ruby", "Silver Amulet with Three Sapphires", "Bronze Torc".

## Materials

Rings are made of copper, iron, silver or gold. Necklaces can be of any metal: copper, brass, bronze, iron, steel, silver, gold, mithril or adamantine. A cord is leather, but whatever hangs from it is metal.

Gems, from the most common to the finest: amethyst, citrine, garnet, onyx and peridot; aquamarine and topaz; emerald, ruby and sapphire; diamond. They are cut round, oval, cushion, pear, princess, emerald or trillion. Only loose gems are found uncut: a setting takes only a cut one.

## Weights

A ring weighs about 10 g. A necklace weighs anything from a gram for a leather cord to about 10 g for a silver chain, 70 g for a long heavy gold chain and 120 g for a bronze torc; a gold torc is over 250 g. Gems add their own weight. Size and metal change these figures.
